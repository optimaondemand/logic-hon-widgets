Lesson 2.02a — Relevance

There are so many fallacies out there that it would be impossible to list them all. Instead, we will group them under three general headings over the next three lessons. Some arguments are fallacies because their premises are irrelevant. Some are fallacies because their language is ambiguous. Some are fallacies because their premises make an inappropriate presumption.

We will start by clarifying the first kind. In order for an argument to be sound, its premises must be relevant. But what is relevance?

What Is Relevance?

In a good argument, the premises should offer genuine support for the conclusion. That means they must be relevant—connected in a meaningful way to what the argument is trying to prove.

A premise is relevant if it helps show why the conclusion should be accepted.

If a premise fails to do this—if it has no bearing on whether the conclusion is true—then the reasoning has gone off track.

Irrelevant Premises: The Heart of Fallacies of Relevance

Fallacies of relevance happen when someone gives a reason that sounds like it supports the conclusion but actually doesn't. It may distract, manipulate, or appeal to emotion—but it doesn't offer real support.

Example:

"You should vote for Johnson—all the young people are."

This premise might make the speaker feel better about voting for Johnson or (more likely) feel worse about voting against Johnson, but it doesn't actually show that voting for Johnson is a good idea. The conclusion might be true, but the reason given is irrelevant.

Why Relevance Matters

When premises are irrelevant, the argument becomes misleading. It can persuade without providing a reason. This is the very definition of a fallacy.

In the next few lessons, we'll look at some of the most common ways arguments fail the relevance test—including personal attacks, appeals to popularity or emotion, and intentional misdirection.

Learning to spot these patterns helps you stay focused on what really matters: Do the reasons given actually support the conclusion?
