Lesson 2.02b — The Ad Hominem Fallacy

If you have ever bickered with a sibling, it probably went something like this.

You: You're wrong!
Your sibling: No, you're wrong!
You: Oh yeah? Well, you're dumb!

At which point, your father might have made good on his promise to "turn this car around." This is a classic example of an ad hominem fallacy. It is a fallacy because it attacks the person rather than the argument. Your sibling would be more than justified to respond like this.

Your sibling: Well maybe I am, but I'm still right!

The point is that the attack on your sibling's intelligence is irrelevant to whether you are correct or not.

What Is an Ad Hominem?

The phrase ad hominem is Latin for "to the person." In argumentation, an ad hominem fallacy happens when someone attacks the person making an argument instead of addressing the argument itself. It's a fallacy because who says something is not the same as whether it's supported by good reasons.

Example:

"You can't trust what she says about climate change—she's not even a scientist."

This is an ad hominem fallacy. The speaker ignores the actual argument and instead focuses on the person who made it. Even if the person lacks expertise, the argument still has to be evaluated on its own merits.

Different Forms of Ad Hominem

There are several ways this fallacy shows up:

Abusive ad hominem: Direct insult
"Only an idiot would believe that."

Circumstantial ad hominem: Attacking motives or background
"Of course he wants tax cuts—he's rich."

Tu quoque ("you too"): Accusing someone of hypocrisy
"You say smoking is bad, but you used to smoke."

Each one shifts attention away from the argument and toward the person making it.

Why This Is a Fallacy

Arguments stand or fall based on the strength of their reasoning—not the character, background, or behavior of the person presenting them.

Dismissing someone's view because of who they are doesn't answer the question: "Are their reasons any good?"

That's why ad hominem attacks are always a sign that real argument has been abandoned.
